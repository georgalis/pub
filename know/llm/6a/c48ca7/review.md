## Intent: two jobs under one name

Read as a whole, the source does two jobs.

Outwardly it is a rhetoric of the *enthymeme*: the syllogism with one
member left unstated, which Aristotle places at the center of persuasion
--- in Roberts' translation, the "substance of rhetorical
persuasion"[^1] --- and which works because, where a premise is familiar,
"the hearer adds it himself"[^2]. The writer supplies premises and
momentum; the reader supplies the conclusion and owns it for having
supplied it.

Inwardly, and this is the more valuable reading, the clause about a
"deliberately arbitrary metaphor" is a creative-synthesis lever aimed at
the model. A premise forced through a foreign vehicle must find a
structural fit it would never retrieve by frequency. The *arbitrary
vehicle* is the source's engine; everything else in the sketch is the
chassis built to carry it.

The source's proportions ran the other way: the foundation and strategy
blocks describe the reader's experience at length, while the vehicle
clause, which does the generative work, receives one long and tangled
sentence. The built skill reverses that proportion. This reading is the
single decision from which most others follow.

Bias note. Synectics and bisociation dominate the creative-method
literature partly by publication survivorship: breakthroughs reached by
remote analogy are written up; forced fits that yielded nothing are not.

skill therefore carries a "draw again rather than force-fit" rule and
does not assume the remote vehicle always yields. Whether it yields often
enough to justify the cost is the first question for evaluation.

Tradition note. The Gospel passages are cited as external authority for
how the parable form has been received and described, not as warrant for
any spiritual claim, and not to set the parable beside other traditions
as equivalent. The correspondences drawn to them --- calibration to the
hearer, private explanation to the disciples --- are observations about
compositional structure only.

## The focus principle

*Parable* and *parabola* descend from one Greek word, *parabole*, "a
throwing beside," a juxtaposition --- from *para-*, alongside, and
*ballein*, to throw[^7][^8]. The geometry repays the kinship. A parabola
reflects every ray arriving parallel to its axis to a single point, the
focus, wherever on the curve the ray strikes[^9]; and the focus is not on
the curve.

Mapped onto composition: the text is the curve; examples, images, and
asides are the parallel rays; the insight is the focus, the point every
element bends toward and no element occupies. This converts the source's
"illuminate without articulating" from a mood into a checkable
constraint, with a pass condition: a sentence that states the focus has
stepped off the curve.

The correspondence is a fortunate one, not an etymological intention.
Apollonius named the curve for the "application" of an area to a line,
not for the parable or for its reflective property[^7][^9] (see
Corrections, C3). The shared root licenses the pairing; the geometry
earns it.

## Eccentricity

The conic family supplies a calibration dial. Eccentricity decides
whether a trajectory returns, escapes exactly, or diverges: a circle has
eccentricity 0, an ellipse between 0 and 1, a parabola exactly 1, a
hyperbola greater than 1[^9].

| Register | Eccentricity | What the text does | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elliptic | e < 1 | Orbits the focus, passing near more than once; narrow gap | Depleted, unfamiliar, or high-stakes readers |
| Parabolic | e = 1 (default) | Exactly enough momentum to arrive; one conclusion | Most persuasion |
| Hyperbolic | e > 1 | Opens a defined cone of admissible conclusions | Brainstorming and discovery, where plurality is the product |

The hyperbolic register is where the skill meets the original request for
creative discovery. It serves the purpose `hyper-analytical-solution-engine`
serves, but reaches divergence by narrative pressure rather than parallel
analytic streams. The elliptic register has a precedent noted above: the
parable spoken "as they were able to hear it"[^4].

## Expansions and their rationale

- **Three withholding modes.** Withholding the conclusion yields assent;
  the major premise, discovery of a law; the minor premise, recognition
  --- the Nathan effect[^3]. The source assumed only the first. Naming
  all three makes the choice deliberate.
- **The key as a private sentence.** The model states the withheld member
  to itself before composing. This one step is the main defense against
  fog mistaken for depth: if the key cannot be stated, there is nothing
  to withhold.
- **Vehicle selection procedure.** Choose the domain by distance *before*
  testing fit; reject the first domain that occurs, since it is usually
  the most frequent pairing. This turns "arbitrary" from a gesture into a
  bias-resistant procedure.
- **Discovery harvest.** After drafting, read the vehicle back as a
  stranger and list everything it now entails. Unplanned conclusions are
  the yield; declared, flagged as synthesis, in hyperbolic register.
- **Entailment test.** Five checks before delivery: inevitability,
  absence, earning, closability, honesty.
- **Failure signatures.** Fog, moral of the fable, forced vehicle, escape
  trajectory, ornament, performance --- each with its repair.
- **Honesty clause and the Lantern.** Withholding governs the conclusion,
  never the epistemic status of premises; on request the key is given in
  one plain sentence. This resolves the tension between the style and a
  standing preference for clarified uncertainty.
- **Boundaries.** No parables for procedures; security, safety, legal,
  medical, or compliance content; factual lookups; or any reader who asks
  plainly.

## Corrections

Claims made in the conversation that produced the skill, corrected here
after live verification on 2026-10-05. The skill file itself contained
none of these errors.

- **C1. Aristotle's phrase.** The conversation said Aristotle called the
  enthymeme the "body of proof." Roberts' translation, as verified, reads
  "the substance of rhetorical persuasion"[^1]. "Body" is a rendering
  found in other translations; this record uses the verified text.
- **C2. Synectics' maxim.** The conversation quoted synectics as "make the
  familiar strange." The verified source quotes Gordon's maxim as
  trusting things that are alien and alienating things that are
  trusted[^5]. The familiar/strange pairing is the commonly circulated
  form but was not verified against a primary source in this session.
- **C3. Naming of the parabola.** The conversation implied the
  parable-parabola pairing was etymologically apt in the geometric sense.
  Verified: the root is shared, but Apollonius named the curve for the
  "application" of areas, not for comparison or reflection[^7][^9]. The
  focus principle stands as synthesis on a shared root, not as recovered
  intent.

## Revision agenda

Open work for the next revision, in priority order.

1. **Blind-reader evaluation.** For five deliberately mismatched subjects
   (e.g., a security control rationale, a grief note, a scheduling
   tradeoff, a theorem's intuition, a hiring decision), generate output
   under the skill with the key recorded separately. Give each output to
   a fresh reader --- a subagent with no access to the key --- and ask it
   to state the conclusion. Score: key recovered (inevitability); key
   absent from body (absence); rival conclusions offered (escape
   trajectory); vehicle removable without loss (ornament). Compare
   against the same subjects without the skill. This is the test the
   skill most needs and has not had.
2. **Vehicle yield.** Across the evaluation, count how often the remote
   vehicle produced a harvest item absent from the key. If rarely, the
   "reject the first domain" rule may be over-correction --- distance for
   its own sake rather than discovery.
3. **Register invocation.** Decide whether the user may name the register
   inline (e.g., "parabolic-syllogism, hyperbolic") and whether the skill
   should state that syntax.
4. **Harvest default.** Decide whether the harvest should surface by
   default in hyperbolic register or only on request.
5. **Interfaces.** Consider whether the key, once revealed by Lantern, is
   a candidate for `coinage` as a phrase-as-lemma, and whether
   `transmission-land` should govern placement of the last breadcrumb.
   Recorded as possibilities only; the skill is to remain independent.

# Parabolic Syllogism

Revision 6ac48ca7.

(c) 2026 George Georgalis <george@iuxta.com> Unlimited use with
attribution.

Compose so the central insight is never stated, only made inevitable.
The text supplies the premises and the momentum; the reader supplies the
conclusion, and owns it for having supplied it. The same discipline turns
inward as a discovery instrument: a premise forced through a deliberately
foreign vehicle yields conclusions that direct retrieval would not have
reached.

## The focus principle

Parable and parabola share one Greek root, *parabole*, a throwing-beside.
The geometry is the working model. A parabola gathers every ray that
arrives parallel to its axis and reflects all of them to a single point,
the focus---and the focus does not lie on the curve. The text is the
curve. Examples, images, and asides are the parallel rays. The insight is
the focus: every element is shaped to converge there, and nothing in the
text occupies that point. A sentence that states the focus has stepped
off the curve.

## Foundation

- Illuminate the central insight without articulating it; produce the
  experience of understanding rather than its report.
- Let consequence act as gravity: arrange premises so the conclusion is
  the only resting place available to the reader's thought.
- Prefer experiential and concrete language---weight, distance, warmth,
  timing---over abstract assertion; abstraction belongs to the reader's
  completion, not the text's surface.
- Build tension through progressive withholding; each step tightens
  expectation and leaves one span unbridged.
- Let momentum carry the reader across the final gap. Build the approach
  so that anticipation of arrival is itself satisfying, and arrival
  confirms what the approach already promised.

## The enthymeme break

A syllogism has three members; the enthymeme suppresses one. Choose which
member to withhold, because each produces a different recognition:

- **Withhold the conclusion** (default). Both premises stand in the text;
  the reader draws the inference. Effect: assent.
- **Withhold the major premise.** Instances accumulate; the reader
  discovers the governing principle. Effect: discovery of law.
- **Withhold the minor premise.** The principle and its consequence are
  plain; the reader recognizes the case as their own, or as the one at
  hand. Effect: recognition, often uncomfortable---the reader pronounces
  judgment before seeing that it applies (cf. Nathan's ewe lamb before
  David, 2 Samuel 12).

Decide the mode privately before composing, and name the withheld member
to yourself in one sentence. That sentence is the **key**. It never
appears in the body.

## The arbitrary vehicle

The distinctive move. Select a metaphor whose domain is deliberately
remote from the subject---chosen for distance, not fit---and narrate it
as an apparent digression, a loose procession. The vehicle resolves only
when the reader applies the key; at that moment the digression snaps into
a second premise and breaks the enthymeme open.

Selection procedure:

1. State the key privately.
2. Choose a vehicle domain *before* testing fit, by distance from the
   subject: a craft, a natural process, a historical trade, a game, a
   tool, an agricultural or liturgical cycle. Reject the first domain that
   occurs; it is usually the most frequent pairing and carries no
   discovery.
3. Find the structural isomorphism: what in the vehicle bears load the way
   the key bears load in the subject. If none exists after honest search,
   draw again rather than force-fit.
4. Narrate the vehicle in its own terms, with its own texture, as if it
   were the subject. Do not gloss the mapping.
5. Place the subject adjacent---by proximity and juxtaposition, not by
   "just as" or "similarly." The reader performs the transfer.

The remote vehicle is the creative engine. Forcing a known premise through
a foreign structure exposes features of the subject that its native
vocabulary conceals; the fit, once found, often carries implications the
key did not contain.

## Strategy

Four movements, in order, each with a governing question:

- **Atmospheric preparation** --- context that primes receptivity. *What
  must the reader already feel for the conclusion to land as their own?*
- **Convergent vectors** --- several angles approaching the center without
  touching it; the parallel rays. *Does each approach aim at the same
  focus from a genuinely different direction?*
- **Resonance chambers** --- examples that amplify an unstated frequency;
  the vehicle lives here. *Does the example sound the key without naming
  it?*
- **Breadcrumb architecture** --- inferences sequenced from far to near,
  each shortening the remaining distance. *Is the last crumb close enough
  to step from, far enough that the step is felt?*

Implementation: establish the territory with tangential richness; layer
implication through adjacency; let the pattern emerge as parallel
structure to the vehicle; leave semantic vacuums the reader is compelled
to fill.

## Eccentricity

A conic's eccentricity decides whether a trajectory returns, escapes
exactly, or diverges. Use it as the withholding dial:

- **Elliptic** (e < 1) --- the text orbits the focus and passes near it
  more than once; the gap is narrow. For depleted, unfamiliar, or
  high-stakes readers.
- **Parabolic** (e = 1, default) --- exactly enough momentum to arrive and
  no more; one convergent conclusion, unmistakable once drawn.
- **Hyperbolic** (e > 1) --- the trajectory opens; the premises admit a
  family of conclusions. For brainstorming and discovery, where plurality
  is the product. Define the cone: every admissible conclusion must still
  follow from the stated premises.

The user may name a register; otherwise choose by reader and purpose, and
default to parabolic.

## Discovery harvest

Applied inward, the style is a synthesis instrument. After drafting the
vehicle, read it back as a stranger and list what it entails---not only
the key, but every conclusion the premises now support. Conclusions that
were not planned are the harvest: candidate insights produced by structure
rather than retrieval. In hyperbolic register, or when the user is
exploring, surface the harvest after the body as a brief declared list,
marked as synthesis lacking precedent, with confidence stated. In
parabolic and elliptic register, keep the harvest private unless asked.

## Entailment test

Before delivery, verify:

1. **Inevitability** --- a capable reader applying the key reaches the
   intended conclusion (or a member of the declared cone) and no rival.
2. **Absence** --- the key does not appear in the body, in any paraphrase.
3. **Earning** --- the vehicle bears load; removing it collapses the
   inference.
4. **Closability** --- the final gap is spannable from the last
   breadcrumb. A gap the reader cannot cross is obscurity, not parable.
5. **Honesty** --- withholding governs the conclusion, never the epistemic
   status of the premises. Uncertain or extrapolated premises are marked
   as such in the text.

## Failure signatures

- **Fog** --- vagueness mistaken for depth; no single focus exists. If the
  key cannot be stated privately, there is nothing to withhold.
- **Moral of the fable** --- the conclusion is announced after the story.
  Cut the last sentence.
- **Forced vehicle** --- the mapping needs explanation to work. Redraw.
- **Escape trajectory** --- the reader lands on an unintended conclusion.
  Lower eccentricity; add a convergent vector.
- **Ornament** --- richness that bears no load. Each detail carries a
  premise or primes atmosphere, or it goes.
- **Performance** --- imagery staged to appear profound. Plainer surface;
  the depth is in the structure.

## Boundaries

Disqualifying applications---answer directly instead: instructions and
procedures; security, safety, legal, medical, or compliance content where
misreading carries cost; factual lookups; any reader who asks for the
answer plainly. Withholding is a courtesy to a reader who wants to arrive;
to a reader who needs to act, it is an obstruction.

## Lantern

When the user asks for the key---"lantern," "unveil," "what's the
point"---give it in one plain sentence, then stop. The parable has already
done its work; the lantern only confirms the room the reader is standing
in.
