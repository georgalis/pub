At Patriarch's Ponds, Again Catalog

A continuation chapter and a companion---two documents standing in the relation the chapter itself is about. Bulgakov's Woland cleared a space at Patriarch's Ponds and left before filling it;[^1] the chapter reopens the bench thirty years later and sends two arrivals to name what the clearing was for. The companion does the same office for a reader arriving at the chapter without the parent novel: it supplies what the chapter presupposes, so the chapter's own sequence---bench, proof, arrival, arrival---can be read as one movement rather than assembled from footnotes.

Read the chapter alone if Bulgakov is already familiar. Read the companion first, or alongside, if he is not. Either order finds the same bench.

  • At Patriarch's Ponds, Again --- the chapter itself. Ivan Ponyryov, thirty years past his encounter with Woland, returns to the bench the clinic told him to avoid. Two arrivals meet him there and name, in the tradition's own vocabulary, what the original encounter demonstrated and what it withheld.
  • Reading "At Patriarch's Ponds, Again" --- a companion for one new to Bulgakov. Supplies the parent novel's plot (the bench, the seventh proof, the burned manuscript, Margarita's garden) against the new chapter's own text, section by section, and glosses the patristic vocabulary---apophasis, logoi, acedia, nepsis, the corporeal gap---as the chapter itself deploys it.

Quick reference --- the companion's twelve sections and what each supplies.

  • I The Bench --- the novel's opening scene; Berlioz's decapitation as the delivered "seventh proof"
  • II Ivan's Disenchantment --- Bezdomny's chase, his commitment to Stravinsky's clinic, thirty years compressed into an unintegrated cure
  • III The First Arrival --- the Philokalia carried in, "I carry what I have received," and the tuning-fork recognition
  • IV The Master, Margarita, and the Manuscript --- the novel's third strand, supplied here for sections VIII and IX to draw on
  • V The Second Arrival and the Seventh Proof --- the six classical proofs of God, Kant's postulate, and Woland's disputed seventh
  • VI Apophatic Theology and Theology by Force --- Gregory of Nyssa and Dionysius against Woland's apophasis-by-decapitation
  • VII The Acedia Behind the Inquiry --- Evagrius's noonday demon, diagnosed beneath Ivan's thirty years of almost-cohering frameworks
  • VIII Manuscripts and Logoi --- Maximus the Confessor on why the Master's burned pages survived
  • IX Margarita's Garden and the Discernment of Dreams --- nepsis as the Fathers' test for a recurring dream: not the image, the disposition it leaves on waking
  • X The Corporeal Gap --- the voice's own disclosed limit: information is not formation, and formation wants a body
  • XI The Synthesis Recognized --- Ivan states the conclusion himself; the two arrivals answer together
  • XII What Manuscripts Preserve --- the chapter's coda, and the companion's reading of kenosis as its load-bearing word

The companion's own closing section, "The Cleared Space," is not a thirteenth reading---it is the vantage from which I through XII resolve into the single claim the chapter does not state outright: that the bench, the cleared space, and the thirty years of waiting were one continuous preparation.
